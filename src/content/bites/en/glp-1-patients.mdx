**For example:** Ozempic, Wegovy, Saxenda, Rybelsus, Trulicity, and Mounjaro (which acts on two hormones, **GLP-1** and **GIP**)

## How does the medicine work?

After you eat, your gut releases a natural hormone called **GLP-1**. The medicine copies this hormone but stays in the body much longer, and it works in four places:

<Cards>
  <Card title="Brain">Sends a fullness signal, so you feel less hungry and think about food less.</Card>
  <Card title="Stomach">Slows down how fast food leaves the stomach, so you stay full for longer.</Card>
  <Card title="Pancreas">Raises insulin only when blood sugar is high, so it rarely causes low blood sugar when used on its own.</Card>
  <Card title="Liver">Lowers the hormone glucagon, so the liver releases less sugar into the blood.</Card>
</Cards>

<Callout type="result">

**The result:** better-controlled blood sugar, lower weight, and protection for the heart and kidneys in certain groups of patients.

</Callout>

## Common side effects

They usually appear in the first few weeks or after a dose increase, and tend to ease with time.

<Chips items={["Nausea", "Diarrhoea", "Constipation", "Vomiting", "Heartburn and burping", "Bloating", "Loss of appetite", "Redness at the injection site"]} />

<Callout type="tips" title="Tips that ease these symptoms">

- Eat small meals, and eat slowly
- Cut down on fatty food, fried food and sweets
- Don't lie down right after eating
- Stop at the first feeling of fullness
- Drink enough water, especially in hot weather
- Don't increase your dose before it's due

</Callout>

<Callout type="note">

If you also take **insulin** or a **sulfonylurea** (such as gliclazide), your blood sugar may drop too low. Ask your doctor about adjusting the dose.

</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Get medical help straight away if you have:">

- Severe, ongoing stomach pain that may spread to your back
- Pain in the upper right of your abdomen, a fever, or yellowing of your eyes
- Vomiting or diarrhoea that won't stop, and you can't keep fluids down
- Sudden loss of vision in one eye
- A lump in your neck, trouble swallowing, or a hoarse voice that doesn't go away

</Callout>
